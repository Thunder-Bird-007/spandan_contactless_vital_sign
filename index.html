Spandan: Contactless Vital Sign Monitoring

Spandan (Bengali: "heartbeat / pulsation") estimates heart rate (HR) and blood oxygen saturation (SpO2) from ordinary facial video — no contact sensor, no deep learning, just classical digital signal processing. It's the term project for EEE 312 (Digital Signal Processing I Laboratory), Department of Electrical and Electronic Engineering, BUET, supervised by Prof. Dr. Md. Kamrul Hasan.

This page is the teaching site for the whole pipeline: six segments that each take you from raw video to a validated number, explained twice over — once as theory you can actually build intuition for, and once as a line-by-line walkthrough of the real, working code that implements it. If you're new to the project, start at Segment 2 and follow the chain in order; if you already know the pipeline and just need a specific piece, jump straight in from the map below.

✓ MATLAB pipeline validated (r=0.957, MAE ~3.8bpm) ✓ Android app defense-ready Classical DSP, no deep learning UBFC-rPPG + VIPL-HR, 149 subjects

The pipeline, in one paragraph

A camera watches a face. Segment 2 finds the face and crops a patch of forehead skin, averaging its pixels into three raw brightness traces over time — R(t), G(t), B(t). Segment 3 cleans those traces up: removing slow drift, then bandpass-filtering to the 0.7–4 Hz range a real human heartbeat can physically live in. Segment 4 combines the three cleaned channels into one pulse signal (CHROM/POS, not naive averaging) and reads a heart rate off its dominant FFT frequency. Segment 5 computes an AC/DC ratio-of-ratios per channel and empirically calibrates it against real ground-truth SpO2. Segment 6 pools every subject's numbers — from both datasets — and runs the same statistical validation (MAE, RMSE, Pearson r, Bland-Altman) a real clinical device study would use, honestly reporting exactly how much data backs each number. The VIPL Integration note explains how a second, much larger dataset was wired into that same, unmodified pipeline to make Segment 6's validation less thin. Everything culminates in a real Android app running this whole chain live, on-device, from the phone's own camera.

Start here: the six-segment chain

Segment 2
🎥

Face Detection & ROI Extraction

Find the face, crop a forehead patch, and turn its pixels into three raw signals: R(t), G(t), B(t) — plus the true sampling rate. The "cheap contact PPG sensor built from a webcam" stage.

 Segment 3
📉

Detrending & Bandpass Filtering

Remove slow lighting drift, then bandpass-filter to the 0.7–4 Hz physiological heart-rate band — discarding everything outside it as noise, by definition.

 Segment 4
💓

CHROM/POS & FFT Heart Rate

Combine the three filtered channels into one motion-robust pulse signal, then FFT it to find the dominant frequency — the actual bpm number.

 Segment 5
🩸

SpO2 Ratio-of-Ratios & Calibration

Compute an AC/DC ratio per channel (Blue substitutes for Infrared) and fit a linear calibration against real ground-truth SpO2.

 Segment 6
📊

Formal Validation

Pool every subject's numbers and run leave-one-subject-out cross-validation, MAE/RMSE/Pearson r, and Bland-Altman analysis — the honest, final-exam version of "how good is this, really?"

 Appendix
🔌

VIPL Integration

An I/O adapter note: wiring a second, 107-subject dataset into Segments 2–5 unmodified, to give Segment 6 a much larger, less-thin subject pool to validate against.

SpO2's calibration is finalized but genuinely weak: with only 8 pooled subjects, its LOSO MAE does not beat a trivial "guess the training mean" baseline within the narrow observed SpO2 range — see matlab/docs/SpO2_Final_Report_Section.md and the Segment 6 Guideline for the full, unhidden breakdown. The Android app's on-device HR accuracy has also not been shown to match MATLAB's validated r=0.957 result (its live buffer window is much shorter than MATLAB's ~80s test clips) — see android/README.md's own verification sections before treating an on-screen bpm reading as validated-accurate. This project reports these limitations plainly because that is exactly the standard Segment 6 itself argues for.
